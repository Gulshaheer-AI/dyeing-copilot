# Concise demo narration

"These are published orders, planned on illustrative machines. I explicitly enable only the listed demo cleaning permissions, then generate and independently verify the plan. The deadline-first comparison is generated from the same orders and rules; it is not a factory savings measurement.

I can compare two priorities: fewer cleanings, or a larger minimum delivery buffer. Their receipts show what was proven and what was merely found within the budget.

Now I select this verified plan as a simulated execution reference at 1 December 2019, 05:00. Work already completed or underway stays on exactly the same machine and at exactly the same times. Started cleaning also commits its immediate batch.

Here is an explicit future Pressure-machine outage. AMD can interpret these words, or I can use structured manual entry. The preview changes nothing. I separately confirm it, and CP-SAT replans only the remaining work under the revised availability.

This summary compares the revised plan with my exact reference. It counts each future batch once when its machine or processing times change. I can inspect both timelines and every preserved operation. The old reference may violate the new outage, so it is history rather than a current recommendation.

The explanation is restricted to computed evidence. I save a supervisor review and export the selected plan with its constraints, receipts, fixed history and verification. Acceptance is for further review, not production approval. Switching the selected option makes the old review stale.

Finally, an outage overlapping protected running work is rejected instead of erasing history. Reset restores the original scenario. This demonstrates simulated planning recovery; it does not establish factory telemetry, safety certification or realised resource savings."
